Wolcott, Colorado

Dr Mark Wolcott
Wolcott, Colorado, is a small, unincorporated community in Eagle County, known for its scenic mountain setting and connection to early Colorado figures like Senator Edward O. Wolcott; it's primarily a residential spot near the Colorado River, with a history tied to ranching and being a small stop along major routes, rather than a booming mining town, featuring historic spots like the Wolcott Yacht Club, now a unique lodging.  

Key Aspects of Wolcott's History & Character:
Location:  Situated at a key spot where the Eagle and Colorado Rivers meet, offering stunning views and outdoor recreation.
Name Origin:  Named for Senator Edward O. Wolcott (1848-1905), a prominent Colorado lawyer and politician who was instrumental in state development, though the town itself wasn't a major center for him.
Early Development:  Its history isn't dominated by gold rushes but by its strategic location for transportation, ranching, and as a small settlement in the Eagle Valley.
Historic Landmarks:  The Wolcott Yacht Club & Historic Inn (formerly an inn and post office) stands as a significant historical building, reflecting the area's charm.
Modern Status:  Today, it's a quiet CDP (Census-Designated Place) with a small population, valued for its peaceful, rural mountain lifestyle, surrounded by natural beauty.   

In essence, Wolcott offers a glimpse into a quieter side of Colorado's past and present, distinct from the bustling mining towns, focusing on natural beauty and community.

Prominent Colorado Wolcotts

from 1982 Denver Reunion Report, Clarissa Wolcott; and from My Dear Friend Chas., Jewell Wolcott
Senator Edward Oliver Wolcott, Colorado (1848-1905) was born in Longmeadow, Massachusetts, third son of Dr. Samuel Wolcott and Harriet Amanda Pope. The family moved to Chicago around 1859. Edward enlisted in the Ohio Regiment of the Union Army in 1864, and was stationed at Fort Saratoga, part of the defense for Washington, D.C. After returning to studies, and then entering business, he entered Harvard Law School in 1870 and received his law degree in 1871. He looked for a location to open a law office and settled in Georgetown, Colorado, influenced by the fact that his brother Henry R. Wolcott was already there. In 1876 he was elected District Attorney and in 1878 was elected to the Colorado State Senate, as was his brother Henry. He was prominent in the field of railroads and moved to Denver. He was elected to the U.S. Senate in January of 1889 and served through 1901. He was very prominent politically and socially, and entertained prominent figures in Wolhurst Mansion, which he built near Littleton. In the State Capitol in Denver there is a stained-glass window with a life-sized portrait in his memory.

Edward Wolcott’s sister Anna Wolcott founded and operated the socially prominent Wolcott School in Denver, along with her other activities in the field of academics and education.

Charles A. Wolcott, born in Ward, Colorado, in 1870, was the son of Horace and Louisa Wolcott who left their home in Grinnell, Iowa, in 1862 and eventually settled in Boulder, Colorado. Charles and his brothers engaged in prospecting and mining, as well as intellectual pursuits. Charles eventually became wealthy and prominent through his mining interests. Jewel Wolcott compiled and edited a collection of letters to him in a book through the Colorado Historical Society: My Dear Friend Chas., Monograph 5, 1990.

WOLCOTT DEATHS

Robert L. Leininger
Secretary

Roger Harry Walcott, 101, Scottsdale, Arizona, passed away August 30, 2025. He was the father of Gwen Sharyn Walcott, Member # 2093L. He was born on February 25, 1924 in Waterbury, Connecticut to the late Carleton and Agnes (Johnson) Walcott. He was the youngest of three children. He graduated from Crosby High School and later Post Junior College where he earned an accounting degree.

He was a proud World War II Veteran who served in the Signal Corps of the U.S. Army Air Corps. As a B17 pilot, he served stateside, training other pilots in Radio Range Navigation for the bombers.

Toward the end of the War, he met Marie Brennan and they married in 1947. They had a full, happy and loving marriage for 66 years until Marie passed in 2013. They had seven wonderful children. Family was of the utmost importance to him. He was a kind, caring and generous father. Roger was a man of high standards and great integrity and instilled these same qualities in his children.

Roger was a CPA by profession. He worked his entire career beginning with General Time Corporation and continuing with Talley Industries after it acquired General Time, retiring in 1989. The family moved to Scottsdale, Arizona in 1970 when Talley relocated their headquarters. Roger loved Arizona and called it, “God’s Country.”

Roger enjoyed working with his hands and was a great problem-solver. If something was broken, he could analyze it and set about the task of fixing it. Even in the last few years of his life he would astonish loved ones as he assembled various purchases he made on-line all on his own without assistance. Amazon will miss him.

For many years after retirement he enjoyed golfing. He followed the local sports teams and was a big fan of Lexi Thompson, a now semi-retired, LPGA golfer. He financially supported many causes through the years such as The Little Sisters of the Poor, Wounded Warrior Project, Disabled Veterans of America and the American Heart Association.

Roger was preceded in death by his parents (Carleton and Agnes), siblings (Ray Walcott and Florence Bramhall), wife (Marie Brennan) and son-in-law (Dave Flake). He is survived by his children 2093L Gwen Walcott (Carol), Rio Rancho, NM, John Walcott (Carrie) Hillsborough, NC, Barbara Flake, Mesa, AZ, Ellen Page (Randy), Los Osos, CA, Susan Bull (Ed), Phoenix, AZ, Nancy Hebert (Greg), Chandler, AZ and Arthur (Sonny) Szczesniak (Elaine), Chandler, AZ along with fifteen grandchildren and eleven great-grandchildren, several nieces and a nephew. The family would like to thank Wes Mitchell at Home with Help and the many gifted and compassionate caregivers who helped Roger in his last two months; Michael J. A. Robb, MD for his care, guidance and compassion when urgently needed; and also Hospice of the Valley, who was an invaluable resource in his final days.

WOLCOTT FAMILY SOCIETY 2026 REUNION

Hosted by: Tera Row; Denver, Colorado; Jul 31st-Aug 2nd
From Susan Parke: Looking forward very much to Reunion-already planning Pirate clothes!
Reunion time is fast approaching! I'm so excited to see new and familiar faces this year, and to share some incredible experiences together. I hope you already have your room reserved at the Magnolia Hotel! It includes a $30 breakfast voucher for each day you stay.

Things to know about this reunion:

Friday, we're going to be participating in a dinner and mystery theatre, Murder on Pirate Island, where we will sail the High Seas with a band of pirate cutthroats in search of the legendary treasure of La Buse. We'll meet Calico Jack Tremain, who claims he has solved the cryptogram that leads to the treasure. Also on hand are Shanghai Ned Brisco from Tortuga, Frankie Five Angels from the Barbary Coast, and disgraced aristocrat, Taffy Leinenkugel. Don't forget Sadie the washerwoman, she seems innocent enough … but there are stories about her … past! You can dress casual or like a pirate! And there is a cash bar all evening.

Saturday, we'll start the morning with our annual business meeting with the twist of being in a room with an actual bank vault! Then we will head to the Garden of the Gods in Colorado Springs. 4 to 6 million people come every year from all over the world to experience this natural wonder. We'll take a guided coach tour of the park and see one-of-a-kind red rock formations and panoramic views. And stop at the Visitor and Nature Center, where we will have lunch and learn about the unique geology and history of this incredible landmark.

At the Visitor & Nature Center we'll experience a fifteen minute high definition virtual ride film, named "How Did the Red Rocks Get There?". The theater uses advanced audio-visual technology to create the illusion we're travelling in a time machine that will take us through millions of years of local geology, from magma to dinosaurs.

After we get back to the hotel we'll have time to share a social hour in the detached ballroom just across the street from the hotel before sitting down to a meal together in the same ballroom.

And then the moment we wait all year for, the exciting cousin against cousin bidding wars that is the charity auction. Please bring anything you'd like to donate to the fray, err…I mean cause. I know we have a lot of crafters. I'd love to see if we could come up with some Wolcott themed crafts.

Sunday, we will travel to Denver Botanic Gardens. Wander through the art galleries in the Freyer-Newman Center, step into the Tropical Conservatory, eat lunch sitting next to the water gardens, experience the sensory garden, and take in the mesmerizing twists of the bonsai garden, all at your own pace.

Alas, all good things must come to a "See you next year!". After returning to the Magnolia the official 2026 Wolcott Family Society Reunion will have concluded, but if you're interested in the unofficial recent tradition of the ghost tour please let me know. Transportation isn't provided for unofficial excursions.

If you're coming early or staying late and have some extra time there is a lot more I wish we could do!

Molly Brown house, home of American philanthropist, activist, and socialite Margaret Brown, who survived the sinking of the RMS Titanic and was known as the "Heroine of the Titanic" for her service to survivors. It's a peek into the interesting architecture you'll see in historic Denver. They even have a walking tour around the area where you can get even more insight!

There is some fascinating street art all over Denver, but especially in the Santa Fe district where the galleries and museums are. For very different experiences The Museum of Illusions is right around the corner from our hotel and Denver has its own Meow Wolf.

We have several craft breweries and distilleries of note. One of my favorites is Stranahan's. The tour includes a very cool shot glass you'll use to taste whiskey, and the people I've taken there that didn't like whiskey left liking whiskey. The guides are usually very entertaining and educational. I also really liked the tour at New Belgium, and I've heard that the tour at Coor's is worth it.

Going a bit further out, you can pick apples at YaYa Orchards in Longmont and have some incredibly delicious apple cider donuts. You can have a relaxing day shopping and dining at Pearl Street outdoor Mall in Boulder.

If you're heading to Estes stop by the Colorado Cherry Company on the edge of Lyons for some local cuisine and jams, jellies, ciders, pies, coffee, chocolate and honey.

Interested in becoming a State VP, but wondering about the job description? The Wolcott Family Society is a big organization, close to 500 members, and it's easy to feel like just another number. For most other organizations, the newsletter would be enough-it's got a lot of stuff in it. But this is a FAMILY society, and that means a more personal connection. Hearing from a cousin in your own state can be a great way to feel a more personal connection.

What's the job description? Pretty much what you want to make it. Since the goal is better connection between cousins, the first step is to let the cousins in your state know who you are, and that the line communication is open. A general letter to everyone telling them who you are, that you'd love to hear general family news and big events such as birth, death, marriage, vacations. Postcards are fun, too.

Why do you want to be one? It's fun, and a great way to get to know some members of your extended family that live, if not close to you, at least in the same state.

Let me know if you are interested-I'll be very happy to share more details.
