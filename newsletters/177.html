PRESIDENT'S MESSAGE October 2018

Holly Miller
Dear Wolcott Cousins,

The Wolcott Family Society is ever changing and evolving. We have now gone to England for the first reunion ever to be held so far away from North America. I'm sorry that not everyone who wanted to go, was able to but maybe we can plan to go back in the future.

Barry and Ann George did a wonderful job of planning this entire trip. There were so many excellent tours and meals planned that it's hard to remember them all (good thing I saved the schedule!) Sleeping in a castle hotel was so special! The accommodations were luxurious, and the staff bent over backwards to be sure we had everything we needed.

My favorite memory of the entire trip was the Sunday morning church service. I walked up a hill on a small dirt road in the rain towards the steeple I could see in the distance. There was a little wooden sign "Tolland Church" pointing me to the left and then big stone and wrought iron gates proclaiming, "Enter into His gates with Thanksgiving!" During this walk the church bells began to peal joyfully. When 1 entered the warm stone church, five bell ringers were pulling on the ropes and continued to ring the bells for about 10 minutes. I had chills as I listened because these are the bells that the Wolcott Family Society donated and dedicated in loving memory of our past President Bill Wolcott several years ago. The church was packed with standing room only as the Bishop led the service. The organ and the voices filled the church with history and worship and love! When the offering was collected, the donation from the society was included. We are not sure at this time what the community will do with the money, but I know they will put it to good use in God's name.

Next year's reunion may be a little closer to home for many of our members. Our hosts Susan and Mark Leyden and Bob Leininger are planning a Presidents-themed reunion in Charlottesville, Virginia. From what we heard at the annual meeting, I think this trip will be packed with lots of fun for all ages!

During the annual meeting we also approved for the 2020 reunion to occur just outside Boston, and Deb(Enos) Adelson will be our host. We'll have lots more information about that in the future so stayed tuned and read your newsletters!

"Traveling.It leaves you speechless, then turns you into a storyteller." Ibn Battuta
Holly Miller
President
The Wolcott Family Society

Interested in becoming a State VP, but wondering about the job description? The Wolcott Family Society is a big organization, close to 500 members, and it's easy to feel like just another number. For most other organizations, the newsletter would be enough-it's got a lot of stuff in it. But this is a FAMILY society, and that means a more personal connection. Hearing from a cousin in your own state can be a great way to feel a more personal connection.

What's the job description? Pretty much what you want to make it. Since the goal is better connection between cousins, the first step is to let the cousins in your state know who you are, and that the line communication is open. A general letter to everyone telling them who you are, that you'd love to hear general family news and big events such as birth, death, marriage, vacations. Postcards are fun, too.

Why do you want to be one? It's fun, and a great way to get to know some members of your extended family that live, if not close to you, at least in the same state.

The journey on Friday in a private railcar on the West Somerset Steam Railway was a highlight for many. A visit to medieval Dunster Castle, where Wolcotts once lived and worked, was highly informative. After lunch in the picturesque village of Dunster, known for its yarn market, the group arrived in Minehead on the Bristol Channel for a short visit before returning to Taunton by rail. Saturday was an exciting day as we bused to Henry Wolcott's birthplace, Watersmeet Farm. Chris and Mary Butler, along with their son, Daniel, welcomed us warmly, showing us the original tucking mill and opening their home for a tour. The group walked the ancient public footpath to Gaulden Manor, where the new owner, Mark Homewood, kindly permitted us to view the grounds. A brief stop in the hamlet of Tolland allowed for a look at two former Wolcott farmhouses, Jays Farm owned by Sharon and Dave Birt, as well as Kay Oxley's Woolcotts. From Tolland, we journeyed to Brompton Regis in Exmoor National Park for a ploughman's lunch at The George Inn.Neighbouring this pub is the Church of St. Mary, a medieval church where Wolcotts once worshipped. Following a lovely drive back to The Castle Hotel through the Somerset countryside, the reunion banquet was held in the private dining room, with many wearing tiaras, or even crowns, in a nod to the hotel's history. To open the subsequent business meeting, the anthems of England, the United States and Canada were sung. Bryon Kirwan, husband of Cyndee Wolcott Kirwan, delivered a highly amusing address to the Swine (Society of Wolcott In-Law Elites). Reports from the business meeting are available elsewhere in this publication. Despite the sometimes heavy rain on Sunday, the service in Tolland's St. John the Baptist church was truly uplifting. Our group of 38 visitors combined with the local population to fill the church to capacity. The church was ablaze with light and flowers to honour both our visit and Margaret, the Vicar's, retirement service. Ruth, the Bishop of Taunton, led the church service and Wylie Johnson, a member of our group, did one of the readings. Holly Miller presented a cheque from the Wolcott Family Society for the preservation and upkeep of the church. A short social at the church was followed by a hog roast at the Village Hall in Brompton Ralph. Again, we mingled with the Tolland locals and enjoyed drinks and a fine meal in their company before proceeding to Lydeard St. Lawrence's Church of St. Lawrence where Henry Wolcott and Elizabeth Saunders were married. Sunday was a truly extraordinary day because of the connection and camaraderie between the local community and visiting Wolcotts. Monday started with a guided tour of the magnificent Wells Cathedral, the seat of religious power during the Wolcotts' time in Somerset. A drive through Cheddar Gorge took us to Abridge, birthplace of John Wolcott of Watertown, Massachusetts. Lunch was enjoyed at The Oakhouse Inn, a pub in existence during the time Wolcotts lived in this village. John Walcott of Shropshire was our guest, but has no connection to our family's lineage. Exploring Abridge's St. John the Baptist church, located next to the site of Edward Wolcott's home and John Wolcott of Watertown's birthplace, gave us the chance to stretch our legs before touring the famous Cheddar caves. Upon returning to Taunton, our tired, but happy group shared one last drink at the Ring of Bells pub before retiring for the evening. A final breakfast together on the Tuesday morning had us saying our good-byes, with promises to meet again next year in Charlottesville, Virginia, from August 2 to 5, 2019. 4 As hosts, we must extend our thanks to the wonderful people of Somerset and in particular, to those in Tolland who helped make this reunion so special. Maureen Canney, the church warden, as well as the Tolland Church Parish Council, including Neil and Caroline Longstreet and Beverlie and Tim Atkins organized the church service and added the extra touches such as the flower arrangements, the Canadian and American flags, and the social following the service. Chris and Mary Butler, Sharon and Dave Birt, Kay Oxley, and Mark Homewood all graciously allowed us access to their homes to see some of the original features. As well, a special thanks must go to John B. Wolcott for his research and guidance on the history of the Wolcott family in Somerset. WOLCOTT LOCATIONS IN SOMERSET Ann and Barry George, Hosts 1. Brompton Regis It was once believed that the Wolcott family of Somerset were descendants of the Walcot family of Shropshire. Later it appeared that the Somerset Wolcotts descended from the Woolcott family of Devonshire, which dates back to the 13th century. DNA evidence now makes both very unlikely. It now appears that they descended from a separate male ancestor who took his surname from a hamlet or property called Woolcott, in the parish of Brompton Regis, Somerset. - Church of St. Mary - a 13 century church where Wolcotts once worshipped - The George Inn is next to the church and serves lunch from Friday to Sunday - Higher Woolcotts Farm has Woolcotts Cross Cottage for rent at www.woolcotts.com 2. Watersmeet Farm The southern part of Gauldon Manor, containing two houses and a tucking mill operated by John Wolcott, was sold by George Mynne to Christopher Wolcott in 1618. It was inherited by Christopher's brother, Henry, who immigrated to America in 1630. The deed described it as "late parcell of Gauldon Manor", so Americans assumed, in error, that it was the house now called Gauldon Manor. The Wolcott land was actually the property now known as Watersmeet Farm. - Watersmeet Farm is likely the birthplace of Henry Wolcott and is a privately owned farm requiring prior written permission to visit. Contact Chris Butler at chris@butler-harvey.com; he is generally welcoming to Wolcott visitors who have contacted him in advance. 3. Gaulden Manor Wolcotts leased land from Gaulden Manor and later owned parcels sold off from the Manor, but none of our relatives occupied or lived at Gaulden Manor until long after we had come to America. - Gaulden Manor has recently changed hands and is undergoing extensive renovations. It is privately owned and not open to the public, so please do not show up expecting to enter the property. The new owner has requested privacy. 4. Tolland - Saint John the Baptist Church - a 13 century church in the hamlet of Tolland is considered a Wolcott family church and is open to the public. The Wolcott Family Society has helped fund it over the last 25 years. The Church Warden is Maureen Canneymaureencanney1@gmail.com . Please consider a generous donation to the church if you visit. 5. Dunster Castle - seat of power and governance during the 1600s, heavily involved in the wool and cloth trade, had connections to the Wolcott family - National Trust property open to the public, beautiful village as well 6. Axbridge - Saint John the Baptist Church - a 13 century church with Wolcott graves -The bank building to the left of the church steps is thought to be the site of the home of Edward Wolcott and birthplace of his son, John Wolcott of Waterstown, MA. When John left Axbridge for America in 1634, his father was the Mayor of the important cloth trading town of Abridge. - The Oakhouse Hotel, near the church in the town square, also existed at that time and is a great restaurant and accommodation spot. Z. Lydeard St. Lawrence - Church of St. Lawrence - a 13 century church where Henry married Elizabeth.
